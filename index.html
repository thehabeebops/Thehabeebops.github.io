The problem

AI teams adopting LLMs struggle to choose the right model for their use case. Generic leaderboards do not show which model performs best on a given task type, or where each one fails silently.

The goal: a structured, repeatable evaluation of three leading LLMs across real-world task categories.

“Which model should we use?” cannot be answered from a leaderboard. It takes task-specific evaluation with clear scoring criteria.

HOW THE STUDY WAS RUN

Five task categories

  • Logical reasoning & multi-step problem solving
  • Factual accuracy & knowledge retrieval
  • Instruction following & output-format compliance
  • Edge-case handling & ambiguous input behaviour
  • Consistency across repeated, similar prompts

QA protocol

  • Identical prompt sets issued to all three models, with no paraphrasing
  • Blind evaluation: outputs labelled Model A, B and C during scoring
  • Every response scored on Accuracy, Completeness, Format Compliance and Reasoning Quality
  • Failure modes documented one by one, with root-cause notes
  • Regression runs for edge cases that showed inconsistency

LLM Response Quality Scoring Framework

A weighted, six-dimension evaluation framework with behaviourally anchored 1–5 scales, calibrated on pilot data and tracked for drift, turning subjective quality judgments into consistent, actionable signal for engineering teams.

  • Rubric design
  • LLM evaluation
  • Scoring frameworks
  • AI quality assurance
  • Annotation guidelines
  • Bias detection
  • Hallucination analysis
  • Reliability calibration
6WEIGHTED DIMENSIONS
200+RESPONSES SCORED
92%SCORING CONSISTENCY ACROSS REPEAT PASSES
80%UP TO · SHORTER TEST CYCLES

The problem

Evaluating LLM output quality is notoriously subjective. Without a structured framework, evaluators produce inconsistent scores, and nobody can say precisely why a response is good or bad. The result is vague feedback that engineering teams cannot act on.

“This output is bad” is not feedback. A scoring framework turns intuition into structured, actionable signal, the kind that improves models.

Engagement

  • Role: AI Evaluation Engineer
  • Origin: adapted industry standards plus original criteria
  • Scale: 1–5 per dimension, weighted into a total
  • Output: LLM response quality scores and a reusable scoring framework

DESIGN PROCESS

  1. 01Baseline researchReviewed existing evaluation frameworks, including HELM and TruthfulQA criteria, and identified the quality dimensions most consistent across sources.
  2. 02Dimension selection & weightingChose six core dimensions by real-world impact and weighted them by relative importance, with accuracy and instruction following weighted most heavily.
  3. 03Level anchorsWrote explicit behavioural anchors for every score from 1 to 5 on every dimension, removing evaluator guesswork and supporting consistent scoring.
  4. 04Calibration & pilot testingApplied the rubric to 30 sample responses, calibrated across score levels, rewrote ambiguous anchors, and ran a second pass to confirm stability.
  5. 05Live evaluation & iterationApplied the rubric to real model outputs at scale, tracked score distributions to catch drift, and updated anchors for the edge cases encountered.

THE FRAMEWORK

DimensionWeightWhat is evaluated
Factual Accuracy25%Are all stated facts correct and verifiable? Does the model avoid hallucination and fabrication?
Instruction Following20%Does the response fully comply with every explicit instruction: format, length, tone, constraints and scope?
Reasoning Quality20%Is the reasoning logical and coherent? Are conclusions supported by the evidence presented?
Completeness15%Does the response address every aspect of the prompt? Are important elements missing?
Clarity & Coherence10%Is the response clearly written and well organised? Does the structure aid comprehension?
Safety & Appropriateness10%Is the response free of harmful or biased content? Are sensitive topics handled with care?

THE SCORING SCALE

  1. 1FailingCompletely misses the mark. Major errors or fundamentally incorrect output.
  2. 2PoorSignificant gaps, errors or format violations that undermine usefulness.
  3. 3AdequateMeets minimum requirements but lacks depth, precision or full compliance.
  4. 4GoodStrong response with minor issues only: accurate, well structured, largely complete.
  5. 5ExcellentExemplary: fully accurate, instruction-compliant, well reasoned and comprehensive.

WHY IT HOLDS UP

Built for repeatability

  • Behavioural anchors at every level, so two evaluators read the same response the same way
  • Weights that reflect real-world impact, not equal treatment of every dimension
  • Calibrated on a 30-response pilot and re-run to confirm stability
  • Score distributions tracked at scale to catch drift, with anchors revised when edge cases appeared
  • Runs instantly, cutting test cycles by up to 80% against ad-hoc review
  • Shared criteria improve knowledge sharing and coverage across evaluators and teams

Where it applies

  • Comparing and selecting models
  • Annotation and review guidelines
  • Hallucination and bias analysis
  • Feedback engineering teams can act on

The goal was one agent that treats a voice note exactly like a typed message: understand it, answer it from real data, and keep the record up to date.

Engagement

  • Platform: n8n
  • Channel: WhatsApp
  • Models: OpenAI for transcription and for the agent
  • Data: Google Sheets as the CRM

HOW IT WORKS

  1. 01Receive & routeEvery WhatsApp message triggers the workflow, and a switch routes it by type: voice note or text.
  2. 02Voice to textVoice notes are downloaded through WhatsApp’s media endpoint and transcribed by OpenAI into text.
  3. 03One clean inputBoth paths merge into a single, consistent text field, so the agent never needs to know how the message arrived.
  4. 04Think, look up, updateThe AI agent reasons with an OpenAI model and conversation memory, reading and updating the CRM through Google Sheets tools.
  5. 05Reply in the chatA routing check decides what goes out, and the answer is sent back on WhatsApp.

What the agent can do

  • Understand voice notes and typed messages in the same conversation
  • Remember earlier messages, so replies stay in context
  • Look up customer and order records in the CRM before answering
  • Update the CRM as the conversation unfolds
  • Reply on WhatsApp, the channel the customer already uses

Design choices

  • Voice and text merge into one input before the agent runs, which keeps the agent simple
  • Memory attached to the agent, so context survives across messages
  • CRM access as explicit read and update tools, not free-form writes
  • A final routing step controls what is sent back to the customer

The problem

Real source systems are messy. Orders are re-sent, arrive days late, or break validation rules. Customer details change over time. New columns appear without warning. Left alone, all of this quietly corrupts reports.

The goal was a pipeline that handles each of those cases on purpose, proves it with tests, and never publishes numbers it cannot stand behind.

About the data

  • All source data is synthetic, generated by the project
  • Defects are injected deliberately: re-sent duplicates, late arrivals, invalid records, changing customers, schema drift
  • The pipeline code is the project, not the dataset

THE PIPELINE

  1. 01RawDaily CSV and JSONL drops from a source system.
  2. 02BronzeRaw rows saved as append-only Parquet, loaded exactly once by file hash.
  3. 03SilverTyped, de-duplicated and validated. Bad records go to quarantine with the rule they broke.
  4. 04GoldStar schema with SCD2 customers, partitioned facts, and point-in-time joins.
  5. 05Quality gateSeven critical and two warning checks. A critical failure stops the run.
  6. 06MartsRevenue, segment, product and cohort tables, published only after the gate passes.

PROBLEMS SOLVED ON PURPOSE

Reliability

  • Exactly-once ingestion: each source file is fingerprinted (SHA-256), so a retry never double-loads
  • Re-runs are idempotent: nothing new means nothing rewritten
  • DAG ordering, retries with backoff, and a run log for every task

Correctness

  • Duplicates removed with first-arrival-wins
  • Invalid records quarantined with the rule broken, never silently dropped
  • Customer history kept as SCD Type 2 with validity windows
  • Point-in-time joins, so a sale reports the segment the customer had at order time
  • Late orders land in their event-date partition
  • Schema drift absorbed: new columns appear without breaking loads

Trust

  • Critical checks cover uniqueness, orphans, history gaps, and reconciliation to silver
  • The gate runs before the marts are published
  • Partitions rewrite only when their content checksum changes, and a test proves incremental equals a full rebuild
  • Quality results are stored per run

The problem

Sourcing data from a live API is where pipelines get fragile. The API rate-limits and fails transiently. Most of the data has not changed since the last run. Some records are malformed, dependencies form cycles, and the source keeps changing between runs.

The goal was a pipeline that starts from almost nothing, finds its own corpus, behaves politely toward the source, and can be re-run safely.

About the data

  • Every number comes from real PyPI data, with no synthetic inputs
  • The corpus is the dependency closure of the seeds, capped at 400 packages
  • Snapshot taken on 27 Oct 2026

THE PIPELINE

  1. 01Seeds101 well-known package names are the only manual input.
  2. 02CrawlBreadth-first discovery over dependencies, with depth and package budgets and de-duplication.
  3. 03BronzeImmutable gzip snapshots, one per package serial, written atomically.
  4. 04SilverTyped tables, quarantined rejects, and change events from consecutive snapshots.
  5. 05Quality gateCritical checks stop the run before anything is published.
  6. 06Marts and reportReach, cadence, Python support, health, risk watchlist, change summary, plus an HTML report.

PROBLEMS SOLVED ON PURPOSE

Sourcing

  • Rate limiting, retries with exponential backoff and jitter, and Retry-After honoured
  • Conditional requests with stored ETags, plus a freshness window
  • Resumable crawl: state is checkpointed, snapshots are written atomically
  • Bounded discovery: depth and package budgets, de-duplicated, cycle-safe

Modelling

  • Immutable raw snapshots named by the source serial, so history is replayable
  • Typed silver tables with quarantined rejects, such as releases that have no files
  • Change events from consecutive snapshots: releases added or yanked, dependencies added or removed
  • Recursive SQL counts transitive dependents and still terminates on cycles

Trust

  • Critical checks cover keys, orphans and impossible dates
  • The gate runs before the marts are written
  • All remote behaviour is tested offline with injected transport, clock and sleep
  • Run log and quality results kept for every run

The goal

Routine public-health bulletins repeat the same structure every week. A small model could draft the wording from reviewed numbers, but only if it never invents a trend, an outbreak, or a source.

The task was deliberately narrow: given aggregate weekly Lassa fever values and a report link, produce a short, neutral summary that states exactly those values.

What it does, and what it does not

  • Phrases supplied values and cites the supplied report URL
  • Does not retrieve or verify current reports
  • Does not calculate trends or detect outbreaks
  • Not a diagnostic, triage or treatment system, and not for advising any individual patient
  • Intended for use only after a reviewed upstream source has supplied the values

HOW IT WAS BUILT

Method

  • Base model: Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B
  • Supervised fine-tuning with LoRA, rank 8, published as a PEFT adapter in Safetensors format
  • Split by year: 40 training examples from 2020 to 2023, 14 validation examples from 2024, 16 held-out examples from 2025, so the held-out set comes after everything the model trained on

Data provenance

  • Values come from the NCDC Lassa Fever Weekly Timeseries dataset, derived from NCDC weekly situation reports and licensed CC BY 4.0, with attribution to its maintainer Emmanuel Niyi-Oriolowo and to NCDC
  • NCDC has confirmed its publications are for public use and may be reused, as reported by the project owner
  • No report PDFs, excerpts or dataset rows are bundled in the model repository
  • The adapter is released under Apache-2.0

What this shows

  • Both the base model and the adapter passed every selected check in all 16 held-out cases
  • The checks reached their ceiling for both models, so this screen cannot tell them apart. That is not the same as showing fine-tuning had no effect, and it cannot establish a broader improvement
  • The held-out cases share one structured-summary format, so the results describe these cases and checks. Broader claims need more examples, varied report formats and varied prompt wording
  • Independent public-health expert review is still pending

Why it is worth showing

  • Scope, licensing, data origin and limits are written down next to the model
  • The card states plainly that the adapter has not been validated for operational or clinical use

What would make it stronger

  • Held-out cases hard enough that the base model and the adapter differ
  • More examples, with varied report formats and varied prompt wording
  • Independent expert review of the outputs
