I’m Olatunji Habeeblahi O., an AI Model Evaluation Engineer and Automation Specialist based in Lagos, Nigeria. My path into AI didn’t begin in a lab — it started with language.

That curiosity led me into Prompt Engineering — studying how language shapes model behaviour, what makes a prompt fail, and how small wording changes can produce entirely different outputs. I began testing systematically, not just intuitively.

Today, I work as a full-on AI Evaluation Specialist. I am adept at independent remote work, rapid guideline adaptation, and delivering insights that improve AI system reliability and user experience.

A structured head-to-head evaluation of GPT-4, Claude, and Gemini — testing reasoning quality, response accuracy, consistency, and failure modes across defined task types.

Methodology
Structured QA · Rubric Scoring
Output

The Problem

AI teams and businesses adopting LLMs often struggle to choose the right model for their specific use case. Generic benchmark leaderboards don’t tell you which model performs best on your task type, or where each model silently fails. The goal of this project was to run a structured, repeatable evaluation of the three leading LLMs across multiple real-world task categories.

“Which model should we use?” is not answerable from a leaderboard — it requires structured, task-specific evaluation with clear scoring criteria.

My Approach

I designed a QA evaluation suite covering five distinct task categories, each containing a set of standardised test prompts. Every prompt was issued identically to all three models. Responses were scored independently against a shared rubric before being compared.

Task Categories Tested

  • Logical reasoning & multi-step problem solving
  • Factual accuracy and knowledge retrieval
  • Instruction-following and output format compliance
  • Edge-case handling and ambiguous input behaviour
  • Consistency across repeated similar prompts

QA Testing Protocol

  • Identical prompt sets issued to all three models — no paraphrasing
  • Blind evaluation: outputs labelled Model A/B/C during scoring
  • Each response scored across 4 dimensions: Accuracy, Completeness, Format Compliance, Reasoning Quality
  • Failure modes documented individually with root-cause notes
  • Regression runs for edge cases that showed inconsistency

Key Findings

  • Claude outperformed across ambiguous and edge-case prompts — showing the strongest ability to flag uncertainty rather than confabulate.
  • GPT-4 led on structured knowledge retrieval but showed higher hallucination rates on low-frequency facts.
  • Gemini showed the most output variance — identical prompts on different runs yielded structurally different responses.
  • All three models degraded in quality when prompts were long, compound, and multi-constraint.
  • Instruction format compliance: Claude followed structured output instructions with 91% accuracy vs GPT-4’s 78%.

Scoring Framework

A multi-dimensional rubric built from adapted industry standards and original criteria — designed to evaluate LLM output quality with consistency, repeatability, and structured insight.

The Problem

Evaluating LLM output quality is notoriously subjective. Without a structured framework, different evaluators produce inconsistent scores — and no one can clearly articulate why a model response is good or bad. The result is vague feedback that engineering teams can’t act on.

“This output is bad” is not feedback. A scoring rubric converts intuition into structured, actionable signal — the kind that actually improves models.

Design Process

01

Baseline Research

Reviewed existing evaluation frameworks including HELM and TruthfulQA criteria. Identified core quality dimensions most consistent across sources.

02

Dimension Selection & Weighting

Selected 6 core dimensions based on real-world impact. Assigned weights reflecting their relative importance — instruction-following and accuracy weighted most heavily.

03

Level Anchors (1–5 Descriptors)

For each dimension, wrote explicit behavioural anchors for every score from 1 to 5. This eliminates evaluator guesswork and ensures inter-rater reliability.

04

Calibration & Pilot Testing

Applied the rubric to 30 sample responses, self-calibrating across score levels. Revised anchor language where scoring felt ambiguous. Ran a second pass to confirm stability.

05

Live Evaluation & Iteration

Applied the rubric to real model outputs at scale. Tracked score distributions to identify drift and updated anchor descriptions based on edge cases encountered.

The Problem

Content is the lifeblood of modern digital presence — but producing it consistently at quality is exhausting. The business needed a reliable way to source relevant content, evaluate its quality, and publish across multiple social platforms without any manual review, rewriting, or posting.

The existing process was entirely manual: find content, read it, judge quality, rewrite for the platform, schedule, post. This took hours daily, produced inconsistent results, and didn’t scale. When the person responsible was unavailable, the pipeline simply stopped.

Manual content handling caused delays, inconsistency, and limited scale. The business needed an intelligent system that could operate continuously — without a human in the loop for every decision.

The Pipeline

1
Content Ingestion via RSS Feeds
Automatically pulls fresh content from configured RSS feeds on a schedule. No manual browsing — content flows in continuously from trusted sources.
RSS · Scheduled Trigger · n8n
2
AI Quality Scoring
Each piece of content is passed to an AI model which evaluates relevance, originality, depth, and audience fit. The model assigns a numerical quality score that drives the next decision.
AI Scoring · OpenAI · Custom Prompt
3
Logic-Based Quality Filtering
Conditional logic evaluates the AI score against defined thresholds. High-quality content moves forward. Borderline content goes to review. Low-quality content is automatically archived.
Conditional Logic · IF Node · Routing
4
AI Content Enhancement & Rewriting
Approved content is rewritten by AI before publishing. Rewriting prompts are segmented per platform — X posts are concise, LinkedIn posts are professional and insight-led.
AI Rewriting · Prompt Segmentation · Platform-Native
5
Review, Approve, or Archive Routing
Custom code steps handle exception routing. Content passing all filters goes directly to publishing. Flagged content enters a review workflow. All content is stored for tracking.
Custom Code · Fail-Safe Routing · Storage
6
Multi-Channel Publishing
Approved, platform-optimised content is published automatically to X and LinkedIn via API. Upload flow control and retry logic ensure reliability.
X API · LinkedIn API · Retry Logic
7
Content Storage & Tracking
Every piece of content processed is stored in a structured database. Creates a searchable content library, audit trail, and enables future automation to avoid repetition.
Database Storage · Audit Trail · Reuse Logic
